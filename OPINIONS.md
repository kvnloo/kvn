# OPINIONS.md

This file is the compact map of kvn's publicly evidenced viewpoints.

## Honest attention and unpaid help

Kevin treats attention as something that should not be paywalled for builders doing real work. He invites people shipping open-source tools or helpful projects to send them for free, candid runs and sharing when they are good, and he asks newcomers to agents to send even basic questions rather than staying silent because they feel behind. He also pledges not to sell his take: paycheck, partnership, or opportunity should not rewrite what he thinks about a product, and he wants the audience to hold him to that if he slips.

## Keep the “small room” even when reach spikes

Even when some posts reach hundreds of thousands to millions of views, he frames ordinary days as a small room and rejects letting large numbers make him colder toward people who take time to write. Trust from long-time readers is the point of the account, not growth theater.
Evidence: [content/2026-08-30_x_some-of-these-posts-have-reached-a-few-hundred-thousand.md](content/2026-08-30_x_some-of-these-posts-have-reached-a-few-hundred-thousand.md) · https://x.com/_kvnloo/status/2094052342836384180

## Agents: check the trace, not just the output

On agent systems with intent contracts, he emphasizes enforceability and observability over trusting a final answer: ask whether the agent invented authority, whether a spawn was allowed, and whether it stayed in budget, with evals and fuller observability (e.g. NeMo Relay) called out as next steps.
Evidence: [content/2026-09-30_x_still-early-but-the-intent-contract.md](content/2026-09-30_x_still-early-but-the-intent-contract.md) · https://x.com/_kvnloo/status/2105358350917452226

## Automation as civilizational leverage and agency

Public Boplog copy frames code as a great equalizer and advances a Whitehead-inspired line: civilization advances by automating important actions and reducing decision fatigue. The through-line is agency—automate humanitarian causes (health, infrastructure, open systems) so competence compounds without gatekeepers, and build autonomous systems so more people can turn ideas into working software.
Evidence: [content/2026-08-15_blog_boplog-about-faq.md](content/2026-08-15_blog_boplog-about-faq.md) · https://kvnloo.github.io/boplog/

## Falsifiable “done,” open harnesses, proof over claims

He publicly describes his X writing as focusing on falsifiable “done,” open harnesses over black boxes, and systems where agents can claim anything but pass nothing without proof. That stance is meant to show up as agent fleets, claim fences, product loops, and experiments that stress autonomous orchestration.
Evidence: [content/2026-08-15_blog_boplog-about-faq.md](content/2026-08-15_blog_boplog-about-faq.md) · https://kvnloo.github.io/boplog/

## Public ledger honesty: commits, not bookmark forks

Boplog inclusion rules are explicit: only public GitHub repositories where `kvnloo` authored at least one commit belong in the archive; bookmark-only forks and private work are excluded. Experiments sit beside products as peer kinds, not as a hidden under-layer.
Evidence: [content/2026-08-15_blog_boplog-about-faq.md](content/2026-08-15_blog_boplog-about-faq.md) · https://kvnloo.github.io/boplog/ · https://kvnloo.github.io/boplog/agents.md

## Evidence-backed OSS contribution loops

`verified-oss-loop` encodes a durable contribution contract: maintainers own direction and merge authority; claims are leased and expire; generation and verification stay separate; every result binds to a revision; workers never merge `main`/`dev`; failure (DISCARD) receipts are useful. The protocol is project-neutral boilerplate rather than an app generator.
